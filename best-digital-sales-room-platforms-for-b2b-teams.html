7 Best Digital Sales Room Platforms for B2B Teams in 2026

✍️ By Julian Blake (Executive Business & Sales Strategy Lead) 📅 September 28, 2026 ⏱️ 9 Min Read 📈 Business Category

A digital sales room is a secure, persistent workspace where buyers and sellers collaborate through an entire deal instead of scattering it across email, PDFs, and shared drives. The category exists because B2B buying has gotten genuinely harder to navigate: buying groups typically run six to ten stakeholders, and 77% of B2B buyers describe their most recent purchase as very complex or difficult. A single shared link gives every stakeholder one place to go and removes friction that would otherwise stall the deal.

Momentum behind the category is real. Nearly a third of B2B sales cycles are expected to run through a DSR by 2028. Below are the 7 best digital sales room platforms to evaluate, each covered in the same depth so you can compare them on equal footing.

1. Projetly

Projetly is an AI-native digital sales room built for enterprise B2B selling, where the same room that runs the deal converts into the customer's onboarding project at close instead of being archived at signature.

  • AI Deal Room, built around four core capabilities:
  • AI Deal Room
  • Deal Engagement: Tracks document views, time spent, and stakeholder activity inside a single branded room
  • AI Deal Insights: Interprets activity to surface which stakeholders have gone quiet or where a deal is losing momentum
  • AI-suggested Next Best Action (NBA): Recommends the specific follow-up most likely to move the deal forward
  • AI-powered Sales to Customer Onboarding Handover (AI Transition Package): Automatically carries notes, documents, tasks, and stakeholder context into onboarding
  • Also includes AI meeting intelligence, built-in sales playbooks, and native e-signatures
  • Limitations: newer to market than several established competitors, and best suited to complex, multi-stakeholder deals rather than simple transactional sales
  • Best for: enterprise B2B SaaS, fintech, and IT services teams that need AI execution plus a clean handoff into onboarding

2. Dock

Dock's core pitch is continuity. It carries the deal room into onboarding as one uninterrupted workspace, backed by native Salesforce and HubSpot sync and solid proposal and video support.

  • Single workspace that persists from active deal through onboarding
  • Strong native CRM integrations and configurable end-to-end workflows
  • AI capabilities are still in beta, geared mainly toward internal sales teams rather than the buyer-facing side
  • Limitations: no embedded CPQ or digital commerce integration, so pricing-heavy deals with configurable products still need a separate quoting tool
  • Best for: B2B SaaS teams with a defined, measurable onboarding process

3. Aligned

Aligned is built for interactive deal execution rather than static content sharing, with AI woven into how rooms and content get created in the first place.

  • AI generates rooms, business cases, and proposals, and recommends next steps directly to reps
  • Section- and user-level engagement analytics show exactly what buyers are reading
  • Deep bi-directional sync with Salesforce and HubSpot
  • Limitations: optimized for pre-sale workflows, with less emphasis on onboarding continuity once a deal closes
  • Best for: teams that want an AI co-pilot embedded directly in the active deal workspace

4. Trumpet

Trumpet's rooms, called Pods, are built for brand-forward customization paired with stakeholder-level AI tracking.

  • Rooms are fully customizable down to navigation, fonts, and content blocks
  • AI deal-health scoring flags at-risk deals at the individual stakeholder level
  • AI drafts executive summaries from call transcripts and helps build business cases
  • Limitations: no native digital commerce integration, and CPQ works only through an API integration rather than as a built-in feature, adding setup work for pricing-heavy sales teams
  • Best for: design-conscious teams wanting granular, AI-driven visibility into engagement

5. GetAccept

GetAccept bundles the deal room with proposal creation, e-signature, and video messaging, positioning itself as an all-in-one alternative to running separate point tools.

  • No-code deal room templates alongside legally binding e-signatures and audit trails
  • AI generates personalized content and summarizes meeting transcripts
  • Broad CRM support, including Microsoft Dynamics and Pipedrive alongside Salesforce and HubSpot
  • Limitations: buyer-side engagement analytics are less deep than dedicated DSR-first platforms
  • Best for: large teams with high proposal volume working across multiple CRMs

6. Highspot

Highspot's digital sales room lives inside a much broader sales enablement platform, so it comes with heavier governance and content controls than most dedicated DSR tools.

  • Rooms can be created and edited directly from Outlook, Gmail, the CRM, or Highspot itself
  • Strong governance, watermarking, and automatic content-language filtering across 20 languages
  • A Deal Agent can auto-generate a room from an existing content item
  • Limitations: mutual action plans are seller-only, so buyers can't co-edit tasks or timelines the way they can on more collaborative platforms, the room itself is one module rather than the core product, and a pending merger with Seismic (announced February 2026, not yet closed) is worth tracking
  • Best for: large enterprises already standardized on Highspot for broader sales enablement

7. Seismic

Seismic ties its digital sales room directly into its enablement content suite, built for organizations that need strict governance alongside the buyer-facing room.

  • Integrates with Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics, and HubSpot, plus meetings and CPQ
  • AI recommends content to sellers based on conversation and deal context
  • Deep governance and compliance controls suited to regulated industries
  • Limitations: content uploads are seller-only, so buyers can't add their own documents or notes into the room, which limits it to a one-directional collaboration model
  • Best for: large, regulated enterprises needing tight content governance alongside the deal room

Is a DSR the Right Investment?

Seven platforms is still a lot of choice, and the honest answer is that a DSR isn't universally worth adopting. It depends entirely on how complex your deals are, and matching the platform to your deal profile matters more than picking the "best" one on paper.

Where it pays off fastest:

  • Enterprise B2B SaaS, fintech, IT services, and healthcare organizations
  • Deals with multiple stakeholders instead of a single buyer
  • Sales cycles longer than 30 days
  • Contracts around $20K+ in annual contract value

Where the value gets stronger with scale:

  • Smaller, faster-moving teams tend to do best with lightweight platforms that prioritize fast setup and a polished buyer experience
  • Larger, more complex sales organizations tend to do best with comprehensive platforms that combine the room with deeper governance, CRM integration, and AI-driven execution

Where it's probably not worth it:

  • Low-ticket or purely transactional products
  • Deals that typically close in a single call
  • Simple sales processes with few stakeholders
  • Cases where setup effort would outweigh the value of any one deal

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a digital sales room? ▼

A persistent, shared workspace connecting buyers and sellers across the deal, housing content, planning tools, and e-signatures in one link that keeps working after it's sent, and in some platforms, after the deal closes too.

How is a DSR different from a proposal tool? ▼

A proposal tool sends a single static document. A DSR is a multi-content, trackable workspace that stays live for the length of the deal.

What does this software cost? ▼

Pricing ranges from free or low-cost tiers on lighter platforms to custom, quote-based enterprise pricing on full suites, particularly those bundled with broader enablement or governance features.

Which platform suits small teams best? ▼

Lightweight, fast-to-launch platforms with simple setup tend to fit smaller teams best, while heavier, enablement-bundled platforms suit larger, more complex organizations.

Do these tools integrate with Salesforce or HubSpot? ▼

Yes, nearly universally, though depth varies, and support for CRMs beyond those two is less consistent across vendors.
